How community gardens share water

Field report · 8 minute read · Updated 14 May

At a glance

Location
North district
Participants
Six garden groups
Season observed
Early summer

Six neighborhood gardens began sharing water schedules after several dry weeks. The groups had different plot sizes, but each relied on a small set of volunteers to coordinate deliveries and maintenance.

A practical schedule

Volunteers marked the days when each plot needed watering and compared those notes with the available supply. A shared calendar reduced overlap while leaving room for a garden to request an extra visit during unusually hot weather.

The schedule was reviewed every Friday. That short review made it possible to adjust for new seedlings, repairs, and volunteers who could no longer attend.

Tools that helped

Groups used a paper map, a shared contact list, and a simple log of water use. The process did not depend on a single app, which mattered for members with limited connectivity.

“The useful change was knowing who had already checked each tap,” said one volunteer.

What remains uncertain

The observation covered one season and a small number of gardens. It does not show whether the same routine would work in a larger district or during a longer drought.
